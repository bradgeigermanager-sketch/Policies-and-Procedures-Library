**Universal Logic of Provenance and Attribution for Communications Origin Analysis**.

Here is a generalized, protocol-agnostic procedure for tracing communication and analyzing a source.

### 1. Procedure: Communication Provenance Trace

*Focus: Establishing the "Where" and "How" of the signal origin.*

1. **Isolate the Event:** Define the communication packet, transaction, or signal that initiates the investigation.
2. **Boundary Identification:** Identify the logical and physical gateways the communication traversed.
3. **Path Traversal:** * Query the hop-by-hop transit logs (the "breadcrump" trail) for the communication.
* Normalize timestamps across all transit points to ensure event sequence integrity.


4. **Terminal Attribution:** Map the terminal node of the communication path to a unique device identity (an identifier unique to that entity, independent of the protocol used).
5. **Validation:** Perform a correlation check—does the device attribute match the communication footprint? If the device is masked or the path is broken, trigger a "Source Obfuscation" exception.

### 2. Procedure: Device Forensic Analysis

*Focus: Establishing the "Who" and "What" on the originating device.*

1. **State Preservation:** Before interaction, capture the state of volatile data. This is the "snapshot" of the device's immediate activity.
2. **Identity Verification:** Map the device identity to its physical ownership or logical registration record.
3. **Action Attribution:**
* Query the activity logs to isolate the specific process or service that generated the communication.
* Distinguish between "system-authored" traffic (OS-level) and "user-authored" traffic (application-level).


4. **Artifact Extraction:** Retrieve historical data (logs, metadata, configuration files) associated with the originating process.
5. **Behavioral Assessment:** Compare the behavior (the communication) against the known baseline for that device/process.
* *If behavior deviates from the baseline:* Flag as an anomaly/unauthorized event.
* *If behavior aligns with the baseline:* Flag as authorized/intended activity.

### Summary of the "Agnostic" Approach

This logic works because it relies on three immutable pillars of investigation, regardless of whether you are in the 1700s or 2026:

1. **Provenance:** The signal must be traceable to a point of origin.
2. **Persistence:** The device must have a record of its activity (logs, journals, or memory).
3. **Correlation:** The activity on the device must logically explain the signal sent.
